# BLOODSPORT — Marketing Strategy
**Target: Gen Z | Twitch & Kick | LoL Community**
*Drafted: May 2026*

---

## The Only Thing That Matters

Honor.

Not as a slogan. Not as a feature. As the reason this tournament exists.

In the film, Frank Dux did not enter the Kumite for money. He did not enter for fame. He entered to honor the family that trained him — a man who gave him everything and asked only that he represent what he had been taught. When Chong Li blinded him in the final match, Dux did not stop. He closed his eyes and finished the fight from memory, from discipline, from something Tanaka had put inside him that no one could take away.

That is what honor looks like under pressure. Not the easy kind — the kind that costs something.

Bloodsport is a League of Legends tournament built around that principle. Not around prize pools. Not around celebrity appearances. Around the question of whether a player can step into a bracket on their real account, against real opponents, and compete the way the game deserves to be competed — with full effort, full respect, and full accountability for the result.

Win or lose, you honor the opponent. You honor the game. You honor the code of Summoner's Rift.

That is all this is. That is enough.

---

## What True Honor Means Inside This Tournament

**Before the match:**
You show up. You prepared. You do not need to say anything.

**During the match:**
You play your best. Not your smurf account's best. Yours. If it is not enough, it is not enough — and you learn something true about where you are.

**After the match:**
Win: you do not taunt. You competed and the bracket confirmed it. That is sufficient.
Lose: you do not flame. You do not make excuses. You acknowledge what happened and you carry it forward. The player who beat you gave you something — the truth about a gap that still exists. That is a gift, even when it does not feel like one.

**The code violation:**
Smurfing is not just against the rules. It is a form of dishonor — competing under a false identity to avoid a real test. The Kumite had no patience for it. Neither does Bloodsport. A smurf account is disqualification. No appeal. No second chance.

Flaming a teammate during a match, intentional feeding, stalling — these are also disqualifications. The code of Summoner's Rift already exists. Bloodsport enforces it.

## The Three Archetypes

Every player who has ever been great at this game embodies one of three expressions of honor. The bracket archetypes are named for fighters who represent each one — not as costumes, not as gimmicks, but as a real description of who these players are.

### The Garen Bracket — Honor in the Way You Win
Garen wins and does not need to celebrate. The victory is in the performance. He plays with discipline, with full effort, with respect for the opponent regardless of the outcome. He would honor a Chong Li the same way he honors a Frank Dux. The fight deserves his best. He gives it.

This is the player who honors every opponent the same — the bottom seed and the top seed, the easy match and the elimination round. Their sportsmanship does not depend on the stakes.

### The Darius Bracket — Honor in How Hard You Compete
Darius does not take matches lightly. Noxus built its empire on the premise that strength without commitment is weakness, and weakness dishonors everyone who depends on you. He finishes every fight. He gives every opponent the full weight of what he is.

This is the player who plays to win in every single game regardless of circumstance — not because they are cruel, but because half-effort disrespects the opponent and disrespects the game.

### The Bane Bracket — Honor in How You Lose
Bane was defeated. He was broken — not by strength, but by will. And in that defeat, he said nothing that diminished what had been built. He was what he was, completely, until the end.

This is the hardest archetype. The player who loses clean, acknowledges the opponent, and carries the loss without excuses. This is rarer in competitive gaming than winning. It is also what separates a competitor from a player.

---

### The Dux Bracket — The Kumite Final
You do not choose this bracket. You earn it. The champion's path. Named for a man who entered an underground tournament to honor a debt, fought blind in the final match, and won not because he was the strongest person in the room but because he was the most prepared — in the deepest sense of that word.

The Dux bracket is the last round. The players who reach it have already demonstrated something. What they demonstrate inside it is the final answer.

## The NEACE Dedication

NEACE spent years telling League of Legends players the truth about their rank when the community was built on comfortable lies. His premise was simple: your decisions determine your rank. Not your teammates. Not your matchups. You.

He did not say it to be harsh. He said it because players who accept that truth improve. Players who reject it stay where they are.

That is the same thing Tanaka taught Dux. Not comfort. The truth. Delivered with discipline and care.

We are dedicating this tournament to NEACE because the standard he held is the standard Bloodsport is trying to maintain. He stepped back from content in late 2023. This is an open door, not a pitch.

---

The bracket as the final word. We are not asking you to endorse anything. We are asking if you want to be part of something that carries what you built. Open door.*

Watches Tyler1, Dantes, thebausffs, DaveyX3. Plays ranked seriously. Wants to know where they actually stand. Has been in enough games to know the ladder is broken. Wants a clean test.

### The Culture Watcher (16–24)
Watches Kai Cenat, Adin Ross, Trainwreckz. Follows competitive gaming as culture. Drawn to genuine moments — the underdog run, the upset, the player nobody expected. Frank Dux was nobody coming in. That is what makes the story.

They need to see something happen before they care. One real moment in the bracket is the entire campaign.

### The Purist (any age)
Takes the Honor system seriously. Would rather lose a clean game than win a dirty one. Has been waiting for a tournament that treats the game the way it deserves to be treated.

This player already believes in Bloodsport. They just need to know it exists.
